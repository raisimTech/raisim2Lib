# Changelog: v2.18

## v2.18.1 (unreleased)

**rayrai**

- The TCP viewer is part of the rayrai library. `raisin::tcp_viewer::ViewerApp` (`rayrai/TcpViewerApp.hpp`) runs it in any program that has an OpenGL context and an ImGui context with the OpenGL3 renderer backend, and `ViewerWidget` (`rayrai/TcpViewerWidget.hpp`) shows it inside a window of another ImGui application, in an ImGui context of its own and with the input of that window. The host decides what the viewer may do with the window (a server's window-size request, fullscreen) and which executable simulates a world file opened in the viewer (`runSimulationWorker()`); the viewer's icons, font and logo are compiled into the library. `rayrai_tcp_viewer` is a short SDL program on top of it, and raisim2Lib's examples carry only that program (`examples/src/rayrai/tools/rayrai_tcp_viewer.cpp`); the viewer sources and icons an earlier sync copied next to it are removed. See [Embedding the viewer in your application](../RayraiTcpViewer.md#embedding-the-viewer).
- The connect prompt of the TCP viewer has an **Open world / robot file** button, which opens a world XML or a URDF / MJCF as dropping the file does.
- Fixed: with split panes in the TCP viewer, `B` toggled the picking-buffer debug view of every pane; only the focused pane reacts now.
- Fixed: dragging the quaternion of a floating base or a spherical joint to zero in the TCP viewer's articulated-system inspector aborted the viewer, because `setGeneralizedCoordinate()` treats a zero quaternion as fatal. Such an edit is dropped and the joint keeps its orientation.

## v2.18.0 (2026-11-23)

**RaiSim**

- **Behaviour change:** the position limits of revolute and prismatic joints act as contacts do. A joint that something pushes past its limit (a heap of limbs, a hard landing) is moved back to it at the error-reduction rate of `World::setERP()` times the violation, no faster than its velocity limit; a limit gives back within the step the impulse that other contacts and limits take over; and it starts from the impulse it held in the previous step. Before, a limit stopped the joint wherever it was, at any depth, could not give back an impulse within a solve, and started every step from zero, so two limits coupled through a light link fought for the whole iteration cap. `atlas_ground` (Atlas falling into a heap) runs 2.9 times as fast (0.76 s instead of 2.18 s); over 40 perturbed starting poses the contact solver needs 41 sweeps a step instead of 64 (fewer on 31 poses) and the worst limit violation is 0.08 rad instead of 0.28, but a heap whose joints were pushed past their stops keeps moving while they are restored (4 of 13 heaps rearranged after 6 s, against 1). A two-link chain resting on two coupled limits needs 9 sweeps a step instead of the cap of 150. `stress_test` (two ANYmals under random torques) is 13% faster and `g1_hand_self_collision` 9%; the other benchmarks are unchanged. `World::resetContactSolverWarmStart()` and checkpoints include the limit impulses. Spherical joint limits, velocity limits and joint friction are unchanged.
- Fixed: a contact warm-started with an impulse of 2e5 N·s or more could skip its first visit in a solve and keep that impulse (a 3,000-tonne ball resting on the ground was launched at gravity times the time step once gravity was removed). The joint limits of heavy systems (effective mass above 1) now count their updates as impulses when the solver decides which rows have converged, so a limit is no longer left moving deeper into its stop.
- Contact solver rows (`contact::Single3DContactProblem`) shrink from 896 to 768 bytes, and the fields a sticking contact between rigid single bodies without rolling or spinning friction reads in every solver sweep now sit in its first five cache lines (the soil-contact check and the second body's Jacobian pointers used to pull in one or two more). Results are bit-identical. On the new `contact_solver_scaling` benchmark the dynamics part of a step is 1-5% faster with 6k to 86k constraint rows.
- Large contact solves are faster. A solve of 2,048 or more constraint rows that runs long (the previous solve took 16 sweeps or more, or this one reaches 16) copies the Jacobians and impulse responses of its rows between rigid single bodies into one array in row order, and the velocities of those bodies into another, and its remaining sweeps read and update the copies instead of following pointers into every body. Results are bit-identical. `contact_solver_scaling` takes 27.6% less time (towers of 4,374 boxes: 47 instead of 67 ms a step; 1,350 boxes: 12.7 instead of 15.0 ms); smaller and shorter solves, and every other benchmark, are unchanged.
- **API:** removed `Single3DContactProblem::pinWarmStart` (nothing set it) and the `BisectionContactSolver::resetWarmStart(const ContactProblems&)` overload that cleared it; call `resetWarmStart()`. `rollingSpinMappInv_i` and `rollingSpinMapp_i`, per-solve scratch of rolling and spinning friction, are no longer members of the row; the solver keeps them.
- Sphere-on-height-map and voxel-map contacts are slightly faster, with bit-identical results: the sphere query no longer initializes scratch it does not read and normalizes a cell triangle's normal only when the triangle is within reach, and the voxel tile caches compare their keys without a library `memcmp` call. ANYmal on a height map is 1-2% faster, on a voxel map 1.3%.
- External forces and torques of articulated systems (`setExternalForce()`, `setExternalTorque()`, thrusters and grippers) reuse the storage of the previous steps' Jacobians instead of allocating new ones every step. Results are bit-identical; `thrusters_grippers` is 7.4% faster.
- Tendons reuse the storage of the previous step's rows, transmissions and inverse masses instead of allocating them every step. Results are bit-identical; `tendon_straight` is 13% faster and `tendon_system` 3%.
- OBJ meshes load 3.6 times as fast. The loader reads the file in large chunks and converts plain decimal coordinates and face indices directly; any other line is read exactly as before, so vertices, faces and errors are unchanged on every platform.
- Depth and segmentation cameras compute their pixel directions faster: each column's and each row's part once instead of once per pixel. The directions are bit-identical; ANYmal with its two 640x480 depth cameras (`anymal_sensored.urdf`) loads in 2.1 ms instead of 4.9 ms.
- Convex hulls of collision meshes (`MeshCollisionMode::CONVEX_HULL`, convexified robot meshes) are built 20-35% faster with identical results: the builder drops removed faces from its scans sooner. With the faster OBJ loading, `mesh_heightmap_contact_check` takes 30% less time, `mesh_stack_plane` 8%, `mesh_collider_speed` 5%, `mesh_collider_heightmap` 5% and `mesh_collider_plane` 3%.
- Fixed: `World::discardPendingStep()` takes back only the thruster and gripper loads of the prepared step. It used to also drop the loads added after `integrate1()` (on a gripped single body, the force added since), bring back loads cleared after `integrate1()`, subtract a gripper's force again from a body made static during the pending step, and a step prepared twice kept the device loads of the first preparation.
- `chain20_speed` steps at 0.5 ms. Its 20-link spring chain, released 90° from every spring's rest rotation, whips at hundreds of rad/s; at 1 ms the default `SEMI_IMPLICIT` integration gained energy until the chain diverged (about 85% of its steps computed with NaNs, in every release since the benchmark was added). `TRAPEZOID`, `RUNGE_KUTTA_4` or a 0.5 ms step let it settle; see the integration schemes in [Dynamics and Control](../articulated_system/DynamicsAndControl.md).
- New benchmarks `heightmap_anymal_random` (with a MuJoCo counterpart) and `voxel_anymal_random`: an ANYmal tracking random joint targets on rough terrain, as a height map and as a voxel map of the same terrain, in 2 s episodes from random starting places. The MuJoCo model gets a free base before it is compiled, so it keeps the URDF's base mass.
- Fixed: the MuJoCo counterparts of `anymal_standing`, `anymal_falling`, `atlas_ground`, `atlas_falling`, `heightmap_anymal_speed`, `stress_test`, `heightmap_lidar` and `world_integration` simulated robots with the wrong base mass and inertia (ANYmal 46.4 kg instead of 30.4 kg, Atlas 181.7 kg instead of 174.3 kg): the free joint was added after compiling, when MuJoCo had already fused the jointless base into the world. They now keep the URDF's masses and inertias, and print them. Their MuJoCo timings change by -8% to +9%.
- New benchmark `contact_solver_scaling` with a MuJoCo counterpart: towers of resting boxes from about 1.5k to 86k constraint rows, reporting rows and sweeps per step, collision and dynamics time, and a signature of the final state.
