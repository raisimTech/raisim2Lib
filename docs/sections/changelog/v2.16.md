# Changelog: v2.16

## v2.16.0 (2026-11-15)

**RaiSim**

- **Runtime joints.** `World::addRuntimeJoint()` connects two rigid objects or articulated links, also of different robots, with a `Fixed`, `Ball`, `Hinge` or `Slider` joint between simulation steps, and `World::removeRuntimeJoint()` removes it again. The joints keep the objects separate; their impulses are solved together with contacts, tendons and joint limits. The world XML (`<runtime_joint>`) saves them with their enabled and broken state, and checkpoints keep the state of the joints that exist; adding or removing a joint makes earlier checkpoints unrestorable. See [Runtime joints](../RuntimeJoints.md). The new `runtime_joint_benchmark` times them.
- `World::discardPendingStep()` cancels a step prepared by `integrate1()` or `integrateNoContactDetection()` without solving, integrating or advancing the world time. It clears the prepared contacts and constraint rows and allows adding and removing objects, tendons and wires again; preparation already done (waking objects, advancing projectiles) is not rolled back.
- Articulated systems use less memory and reuse their dynamics scratch storage between steps. `MatDyn` copies no longer allocate for empty matrices.
- Windows: the native FEM mesher build compiles in RaiSim's message logger, and its external projects use explicit build directories.
- The FEM mesher reads and tessellates more STEP files; six complex parts (a DC motor, a hollow section, a pneumatic cylinder, a servo horn, a socket screw and a sprocket) are checked against independent Open CASCADE measurements, and a knee mount is a browser regression case.
- The `raisim_engine2` world editor (the `raisim_engine2/` tree and the `RAISIM_ENGINE2` CMake option) was removed from this repository.

**raisimPy**

- `raisimpy.RuntimeJoint` and `World.addRuntimeJoint()`, `getRuntimeJoint()`, `getRuntimeJoints()` and `removeRuntimeJoint()`; the `runtimeJoints.py` example.
- `World.discardPendingStep()`.

**Examples and documentation**

- The C++ `runtime_joints` example.
- The examples build with MSVC's `/bigobj`.
- The Newton benchmark pages compare ten-step control intervals and random actuation, and normalize performance by hardware price.
